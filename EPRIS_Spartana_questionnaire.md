# EPRIS Journal — Interview Questionnaire
**Stephen Spartana, photographer (Baltimore, MD)**

Dear Stephen,

Thank you for agreeing to speak with EPRIS Journal. We have spent time with your portfolio, your CV and your series, and prepared thirteen questions. Each comes with a little context, so you can see what prompted it. That context was gathered from public sources and may be imprecise or put together in the wrong order, so please correct us wherever it is not how it happened. Your own account always takes the place of ours. Answer at whatever length feels right, and feel free to skip or reshape any question. Images that accompany your answers are very welcome.

---

### 1. From the baseball field to the Melt Shop
You went to college intent on becoming a baseball player. You studied economics and accounting, but never saw them as a career, and took your first photography class only as a senior. Later you worked at a steel mill to make money and began taking art courses at a community college. That is when you realised this was what you wanted to do, and you applied to MICA, working long hours at the mill to pay for art school, and often thinking how interesting it would be to photograph in the Melt Shop. Your first shows in Baltimore, "Aerial" at Nye-Gomez Gallery (1992) and "Human-Nature" at Gomez Gallery (1994), came later.
**What was it like to arrive at photography by such a long road: baseball, economics, the steel mill, then art school? Do you think the mill, or the field, still shows in the way you see? Did you ever get to photograph in the Melt Shop? And was there one image, class or moment you would call the true beginning of your artistic voice?**

### 2. Time and space as a subject
You describe your goal as expanding your research into "the relationship of time and space." It's a phrase more often heard from physicists than photographers, and it is interesting that your 2019 exhibition "Intertwined" was held at the American Center for Physics.
**What does "time and space" mean for you, concretely, when you stand behind the camera? How do you make time visible in a medium that, by definition, freezes a single instant?**

### 3. Venice and "Drifting Through Time"
In 2021 you took part in the "Time, Space" exhibition in Venice (May to October), and your CV also lists "The Perspective of Color" at Spazio Gallery in Venice. Venice also has its own gallery on your website. Your series "Drifting Through Time" received an Honorable Mention at the Budapest International Foto Awards in 2020.
**What did you show in Venice in 2021, and what draws your work to that city? Venice is built on water and has been slowly sinking through time; how does it speak to your research on time and space?**

### 4. Human and nature, intertwined
"Human-Nature", "Human-Nature: Life in Layers", "Intertwined", "Interwoven", "Rooted", "Hand-Up": across your career, the human and the natural world keep growing into each other.
**Is this intertwining a vision of harmony, of dependence, or of tension? As climate and ecology have become urgent public topics, has the meaning of these series changed for you, or for your audience?**

### 5. The layer as method and metaphor
"Life in Layers" won first place at the Tokyo International Foto Awards in 2019, and "Nature in Layers" a Bronze there in 2021. Layering seems to be central to your language.
**Please take us inside the technique: how is a layered image built, in camera or in post-production, and how do you know when it is finished? And beyond technique, what does a layer stand for: memory, the passage of seasons, the coexistence of several moments in one?**

### 6. Ice and the world below zero
In 2022 a cold body of work brought you several awards: "Cryos-Scapes" (Gold, Budapest International Foto Awards 2022), "Ice Scapes" and "World Below 0°".
**Where were these images made, and what drew you to ice? Ice is time made visible, water stopped mid-motion and slowly melting. Is this series a continuation of your research on time, a meditation on a disappearing world, or something else entirely?**

### 7. The thin line between photograph and abstraction
Many of your recent prizes are in Fine Art Abstract: "Whisper in the Woods" (Gold, Jury Top 5, Tokyo 2024), "Yin Yang", "Ethereal Entity", "Night Hat", "Anima". Yet your sources are real trees, leaves, water and light.
**At what point does a photograph of a forest stop being a forest and become an abstraction? Do you set out to abstract, or does it reveal itself in the image? And how do you feel when viewers cannot tell your photographs from paintings?**

### 8. The object: metal, paper, fabric
You print on metal, paper and various fabrics (your collection "Strati" even turns photographs into silk scarves and wraps), and you work in both digital and analogue photography, including a Silver at the Sparks Analog International Awards in 2023.
**How does the physical surface change the meaning of an image? Could you describe a specific work where the choice of material, say metal versus fabric, completely transformed what the photograph says? And in an age when most images live on screens, why does the physical print still matter to you?**

### 9. Traveller and fine-art photographer
Alongside your abstract series, your portfolio holds Venice, Italy, Nature-Europe, Asia and "Elephants of Sri Lanka". In 2018 you showed "Blue" in "Asia in Maryland: Expressing Cross-Culture Experience".
**How do these two sides of your practice, documenting places and creatures and dissolving reality into abstraction, feed each other? What did Asia, and the elephants of Sri Lanka in particular, teach you as a photographer and as a person?**

### 10. A global stage in a digital age
2025 was an extraordinary year: the Florence Biennale, where you received the "Lorenzo il Magnifico" Award for Photography (4th Prize), and also Cologne Unframed, Swiss Artexpo, Art Basel, the Carrousel du Louvre, Berlin, Dubai and Palma, many of them in digital format.
**What did it feel like to be honoured by a prize named after Lorenzo de' Medici in the city of the Renaissance? And as someone who has exhibited both in physical galleries and on digital screens, what is gained and what is lost when photographs are shown digitally? Do international awards change how you work, or only how you are seen?**

### 11. Baltimore and community
Much of your exhibition life has grown in Maryland: the Creative Alliance, InSquare Art (a gallery you co-founded and owned), Raw Ink Gallery, the Himmelfarb Gallery, Blackrock Center for the Arts, and juried shows at the d'Art Center in Norfolk, Virginia.
**What role has the Baltimore art community played in your development? What did it mean to run a gallery yourself, and how does a local scene shape an artist who now shows worldwide? What does home give you that biennales cannot?**

### 12. The book
You mentioned that you are working on a book with an online publishing company, and that it has been "an interesting adventure." Your website announces it as *Beyond the Photograph*, with full-bleed reproductions of more than a hundred works.
**Whatever you are ready to share: what story do you want the book to tell, and how has putting your work into the sequence and permanence of a book changed the way you look at it?**

### 13. Art in a time of war
EPRIS is edited in Ukraine, a country living through war, where time and space, home, distance and the fragility of the natural world have acquired a sharp and painful meaning.
**What can art, and photography especially, offer people living through destruction and uncertainty? And if you could send one of your images to readers in Ukraine, which would it be, and what would you want them to feel when they see it?**
