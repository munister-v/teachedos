📖 Facts about The Ukrainian Khata — Village Home

🎨 Whitewash before holidays

Before Easter and Christmas the khata was whitewashed inside and out, the clay floor smeared fresh, and clean rushnyky hung on the icons. The whole village glowed white at holidays — cleanliness was a form of celebration.

🌸 A garden with meanings

Nothing by the khata was planted 'just because': mallows for beauty and memory, marigolds against pests and for songs, viburnum by the well, rue and mint by the door. The flowerbed was a pharmacy, a spice rack, and a poem at once.

🪣 The well as a mirror

A girl's song tradition sent her to the well at dawn: the first water, drawn before anyone else, was 'unspoiled' — used for washing, for dough, and, in fortune-telling jokes, for seeing a future husband's reflection. Handle with care.

🧵 Faceless on purpose

The motanka's empty face frightens modern doll-lovers — until they hear why: a doll with a face might 'see' and judge; the faceless one only protects. It is one of the oldest Ukrainian crafts, wound by hand for over a century of grandmothers.
