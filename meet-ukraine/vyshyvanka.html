📖 Facts about Vyshyvanka — The Embroidered Shirt

🗓️ Vyshyvanka Day

On the third Thursday of May, people in Ukraine and far beyond wear vyshyvanky to work, to school, and to the office. Streets fill with embroidery — it is a working day, not a holiday, and that is the point: heritage worn inside ordinary life.

🧵 A coded language

The eight-pointed star means the sun and balance; diamonds mean a sown field and fertility; wavy lines mean water; hop cones mean youth and love. A mother stitching a shirt for her son was literally writing him a letter of wishes.

🛡️ Embroidery as armour

Shirts were embroidered for soldiers, travellers, and babies — for anyone leaving home. The belief was simple: while your mother's stitches are on your chest, home itself is protecting you, wherever you are.

👵 Grandmother's chest

In many families the oldest vyshyvanky are kept in a chest (skrynia) and handed down. A shirt can outlive its maker by a hundred years — grandchildren wear great-grandmother's stitches to weddings today.
